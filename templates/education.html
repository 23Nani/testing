Understanding LWE Encryption

What is Learning With Errors (LWE)?

LWE is a post-quantum cryptographic system that secures data against both classical and quantum computer attacks.

Core Concept

Security relies on the hardness of solving "noisy" linear equations. Authorized users can remove the mathematical noise; attackers cannot.

Key Difference: Unlike RSA (factoring) or ECC (discrete logarithms), LWE's security doesn't collapse under quantum attacks!

Mathematical Foundation

Polynomial Ring Arithmetic

LWE operates in the ring: \( R_q = \mathbb{Z}_q[x]/(x^n+1) \)

  • Modular Algebra: All operations are modulo \( q \) (a prime number)
  • Polynomial Rings: Polynomials of degree up to \( n-1 \)
  • Reduction: \( x^n = -1 \), so \( x^{n+k} = -x^k \)
Key Generation

1. Choose secret key: \( s \in R_q \) (small polynomial)
2. Sample random: \( a \in R_q \)
3. Sample error: \( e \) from discrete Gaussian distribution
4. Compute: \( b = a \cdot s + e \pmod{q} \)

Public Key: \( (a, b) \)     Private Key: \( s \)

Encryption

To encrypt message \( m \):
1. Choose random: \( r \in R_q \) (small polynomial)
2. Sample errors: \( e_1, e_2 \)
3. Compute ciphertext:
  \( u = a \cdot r + e_1 \)
  \( v = b \cdot r + e_2 + m \cdot \lfloor q/2 \rfloor \)

Decryption

To decrypt ciphertext \( (u, v) \) using secret key \( s \):
\( m' = v - u \cdot s \approx m \cdot \lfloor q/2 \rfloor + \text{(small noise)} \)
Round to recover \( m \)
